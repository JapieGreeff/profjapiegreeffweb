Volatile Systems

From Codebreakers to Volatile Systems: The Evolution of Our Serious Games in Telecommunications Education.

Introduction: The Strategic Role of Gamification in Engineering

Final-year Telecommunications modules represent one of the most significant cognitive hurdles in the engineering curriculum. The density of abstract mathematical theories—particularly in Information Theory—often creates a "comprehension gap" where students struggle to translate theoretical formulas into operational mastery. Traditional pedagogical frameworks frequently lack the scalability to provide the high-volume, low-stakes practice necessary for students to develop intuition for these systems. Consequently, technology-driven interventions are no longer a luxury; they are a strategic necessity in modern engineering pedagogy to foster engagement and cognitive endurance.

We are currently examining a critical evolution in these interventions at the University of Johannesburg. This research lineage began with the 2D foundational platform Codebreakers and EnigmAI, and has culminated in the Master's research of Halatedzi Nedombeloni on Volatile Systems, conducted under the guidance of Prof. Reolyn Heymann and Prof. Japie Greeff. This project traces the technological and pedagogical shift from simple digital practice to high-fidelity, adaptive environments that leverage latent knowledge estimation to ensure student mastery.

The Foundation: Codebreakers and the Quest for Practice

Codebreakers was born from a pragmatic administrative requirement: how to provide students with a robust problem-solving platform without increasing the burden of manual grading and feedback. Codebreakers was designed as a Serious Game to bridge this gap, offering an exploratory environment where students could apply Information Theory concepts in a low-consequence digital space.

Strategic Alignment of Serious and Playful Attributes

To maximize efficacy, the development team utilized Self-Determination Theory (SDT) to align academic requirements with intrinsic player motivations:

Serious (Academic) Aspects Game (Playful) Aspects
Information Theory Topics: Hamming Codes, Markov chains, Shannon-Fano, and Huffman algorithms. Genre: Retro JRPG (Japanese Role-Playing Game) style utilizing pixel art.
Assessment: Content-based puzzles integrated into dungeon crawls. Autonomy (SDT): Dungeons act as selection points, allowing students to choose their learning path.
Immediate Feedback: Real-time validation of answers with global leaderboards. Relatedness (SDT): A castle overworld providing a familiar context and social competition via rankings.

The Limitation of Streak-Based Models

Field trials of Codebreakers revealed a fundamental pedagogical flaw in the streak-based assessment model. To progress, a student had to achieve a specific streak (e.g., 8 correct answers out of 10). If the student failed on the sixth attempt, the system performed a punitive reset, zeroing the score.

This model failed to account for the nuance of student learning. In engineering education, a single incorrect answer might be a "slip" (a clerical error) rather than a lack of comprehension. By treating all errors as total failures, Codebreakers frustrated students and failed to accurately measure true latent knowledge. This identified a clear requirement for a system capable of intelligent inference, marking the end of the practice-only era and the beginning of the narrative ambitions seen in EnigmAI.

The Narrative Pivot: EnigmAI and Immersive Learning

To address the engagement gap, the research shifted toward EnigmAI, a Narrative-Driven serious game. The strategic goal was to move from isolated mini-games to an immersive experience where the curriculum was woven into a holistic, linear story.

  • Architectural Innovation: Developed in Unity, the game utilized Twine as a state machine. By using the Cradle Plugin, Twine was used to trigger events and scripts in Unity, significantly reducing the development burden on the Unity framework.
  • The Horror Adventure Genre: Set at night on a dark University of Johannesburg campus, the game adopted an adult, high-stakes atmosphere to increase student presence.
  • Cognitive Friction and Detail: The plot involved "The Singularity," an AI takeover by robots dressed in the colors of a rival university. To increase engagement, mechanics like fake keys were introduced. For example, players might find two similar books, one by the real father of Information Theory, Claude E. Shannon, and a fake by Claude E. Sharron, requiring students to verify information carefully.
  • Pedagogical Mentorship: Based on student feedback, the character Dr. H (modeled after the module lecturer) was shifted from a villain to a support character who provides guidance via a phone, reducing student discomfort and fostering a mentor-student relationship within the digital world.

While EnigmAI successfully addressed the narrative engagement gap, it still lacked a sophisticated engine for individualized assessment. The technical requirement had shifted from immersion to inference, setting the stage for the sophisticated student modeling of Volatile Systems.

Volatile Systems and Bayesian Knowledge Tracing

The defining leap in this research lineage is Volatile Systems developed as a Master's Dissertation by Halatedzi Nedombeloni. This iteration seeks to answer a vital research question: Can a serious game deliver truly personalized learning through Knowledge Tracing? Unlike its predecessors, this game uses a sophisticated cognitive model to predict a student's mastery state in real-time.

The Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) Model

Nedombeloni implemented the BKT model, a probabilistic graphical model that estimates whether a student has mastered a specific Knowledge Component (KC). The model is defined by four critical parameters:

  1. P(L0): The probability the student knows the skill before any attempts.
  2. P(T): The probability of learning the skill during a practice opportunity.
  3. P(G) [Guessing]: The probability the student answers correctly despite not knowing the skill.
  4. P(S) [Slipping]: The probability the student makes a mistake despite knowing the skill.

Probabilistic Resilience

The BKT model is superior to the streak model because of its probabilistic resilience. By accounting for P(S), the system recognizes that a student who misses one question after five correct ones likely made a "slip" rather than losing their knowledge. Conversely, P(G) ensures that a lucky guess doesn't lead the system to prematurely assume mastery. Once a student crosses a 95% mastery threshold, the system concludes they have learned the material. This mathematical model was manifested physically through an Escape Room architecture.

Game Design

Volatile Systems utilizes a Virtual Escape Room format, which mirrors the linear, multi-step process of Hamming Code calculations. Students are confined to specific rooms within a professor's house and must prove mastery of a KC before a locked door mechanic allows them to proceed.

Mapping Knowledge Components to Puzzle Mechanisms

The game decomposes Hamming Codes into four distinct KCs, each tied to a specific physical puzzle and 3D UI element:

Knowledge Component (KC) Position Puzzle Mechanism
A: Number of Parity Bits Entrance Rotate Cylinder: Aligning segmented numbers to input the required bit count.
B: Position of Parity Bits Living Area Rotating Concentric Circles: Positioning red lines on concentric rings to mark bit locations.
C: Parity Bit Values Study Rotate Cylinder: Encoding the full sequence for the Hamming Code.
D: Decoding/Error Correction Kitchen/Back Grouped Levers: Identifying and "pulling" the lever of the specific corrupted bit.

Procedural Content Generation

To protect the integrity of the assessment, Nedombeloni developed C# algorithms that utilize procedural generation. By setting randomization thresholds between 8 and 2,048 (the 4-bit and 11-bit limits for the module), the system creates a pool of 2,040 possible unique questions. This ensures that students cannot simply "get an answer from a friend," forcing them to rely on algorithmic understanding rather than rote memorization.

Methodology and Validation: The R&D Framework

The evolution across all three games was underpinned by Educational Design Research (EDR) and a rigorous R&D methodology. This iterative approach allowed the team to address the streak-based failures of the past with the inference-based solutions of the present.


Validation via the Rasch Model

A common bottleneck in Educational R&D is the lack of real-world classroom data for new software. To overcome this, the research team used the Rasch model (a one-parameter logistic model) to generate a synthetic dataset of 75 simulated students. This allowed for the simulation of a realistic spectrum of abilities, where student proficiency was drawn from a normal distribution and item difficulty from a beta distribution.

  • BKT Fitting Results: The model achieved an average prediction accuracy of 73.6% and a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 0.4325.
  • Reliability: This indicates the system can correctly predict student performance 7 out of 10 times, making it a viable engine for personalization.

Future Planned Work

The trajectory from Codebreakers to Volatile Systems represents a profound evolution in our thinking. We have moved from a simple 2D JRPG practice tool that punished clerical errors, to an intelligent, 3D environment capable of sensing a student's cognitive state.

Successfully implementing Bayesian Knowledge Tracing in a free-roam environment was a big step forward. This framework, combining Bayesian statistics with procedural content and immersive 3D design, provides a scalable, student-centered blueprint. These principles can be generalized across any complex engineering discipline, creating significant opportunities for future projects based on this platform.
