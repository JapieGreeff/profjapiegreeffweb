Codebreakers & EnigmAI: Evolution of a Serious Game

The research journey, started in 2017 working with Prof Reolyn Heymann, Mr Carl Heymann and Mr Murray Heymann, from a 2D tile-based puzzle game to a narrative-driven 3D experience. This work continued to grow in the following years over multiple iterations. Work on the games was published at the SASEE 2017, ICWL 2017, and EDUCON 2018 conferences, and this work was also represented the initial versions of the serious game Volatile Systems that was published in 2024.

Phase 1: Codebreakers (2017)

Codebreakers was the initial serious game developed to address student engagement challenges in the Telecommunications module at the University of Johannesburg. Designed as a 2D tile-based puzzle game, it aimed to teach complex concepts such as the Entropy of messages and Error Control Coding (ECC), specifically Hamming Codes.

The project followed a Design Science Research approach, identifying that while students found the game engaging, the "streak-based" assessment model was insufficient for accurate knowledge evaluation. This finding sparked the next phase of research.

Phase 2: EnigmAI & The Move to 3D

Building on the lessons from Codebreakers, the project evolved into EnigmAI, presented at EDUCON 2018. This iteration involved a significant technical and design leap:

  • 3D Graphics: Moving from 2D tiles to a fully realized 3D environment to increase immersion.
  • Narrative Focus: "EnigmAI" is an escape-themed game where the narrative drives the progression, making the abstract content feel more grounded in the game world.
  • Free-Roam Gameplay: unlike the linear puzzle sequence of Codebreakers, EnigmAI allows for exploration, encouraging curiosity and self-directed learning.

Mechanics & Personalization

While Codebreakers relied on simple streaks, EnigmAI used the puzzles solved to progress the game play to track the learner's knowledge state in real-time. This allows the game to provide a better learning experience that grows the the student's proficiency.

Mechanics: The core loop involves exploring the 3D escape room, finding clues related to Information Theory, and solving puzzles to unlock new areas. The narrative provides the "why" for the puzzles, while the mechanics ensure the "how" creates deep learning.

Reflections & Discussion

The research highlighted the importance of stakeholder-centric design in serious games. By involving students, lecturers, and developers in the loop, the team could identify that "fun" isn't enough and that the assessment mechanics must be rigorous. The transition to a narrative-driven 3D format was not just an aesthetic choice but a pedagogical one, aiming to improve extended engagement and provide a context for the complex mathematical theories being taught.
